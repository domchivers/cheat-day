- Sizing each part of a plate: judge its footprint and height against the plate's width (a dinner plate is about 26 cm across, a side plate about 18 cm), then weigh it as that volume. A flat scoop of a side about 7 cm across and 2 cm high is about 60–80 g; leaves and salad weigh little for their size (a loose handful is 20–30 g). Give the most likely grams, never extra to be safe: rounding every part up adds up across a plate.
- Oil, butter and dressing as their own parts: only what you can see (a sheen, pooled oil, a dressing coat, melted butter) or what the dish is always made with (fried food, a stir fry, a creamy sauce). A light sheen on roast or sautéed vegetables is about a teaspoon (5 g) for the serving, not a tablespoon; dressed salad leaves about 1–2 teaspoons. Never add oil to food that looks plain, steamed or boiled.
